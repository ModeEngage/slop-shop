# Execute a Prototype Issue

Build a cheap, rough, concrete artifact that helps a human assess a concept. The assignment includes collecting feedback
and recording what was learned.

## Build and Collect Feedback

Establish what the artifact should help clarify, who will provide feedback, and any known scope or effort limits.
Use the available assignment and context before asking for missing information.

Choose the simplest artifact that lets the human assess the relevant behavior or idea. An outline, mockup, stub, or
working code can be appropriate. Match fidelity to the feedback needed and follow the assignment's limits.

Present the artifact and explain limitations that affect how the human should interpret it. Collect actual feedback;
do not substitute the agent's assessment for the human's response. If the participant is unavailable, record that
feedback remains pending.

As part of each feedback round, establish whether the human wants another iteration or considers the prototype work
complete. Use an explicit decision already present in their feedback; ask only when it is unclear. Silence or positive
feedback alone does not establish completion.

When another iteration is requested, identify the changes and what they should clarify, then revise the artifact and
return it for review. Follow the assignment's scope and effort limits. If the requested iteration exceeds those limits,
resolve the scope or limit change with the human before continuing. If the human considers the work complete, record
that decision and any remaining questions.

## Findings

Record the artifact or a durable link, the feedback received, the human's iteration or completion decision, what was
learned, and unresolved questions. Distinguish human feedback from the agent's interpretation.

Use the location specified by the assignment. If none is specified, return the findings to the user.
For work assigned through Linear, read [references/linear-findings.md](references/linear-findings.md).

## Completion

Completion requires the human's decision that prototype work is complete, recorded findings, and satisfaction of any
additional requirements in the assignment. Keep the assignment incomplete while review, a requested iteration, or the
human's completion decision remains pending. Reaching an effort limit alone does not establish successful completion.

A completed prototype is not evidence that the artifact is ready for product use. Record any follow-up work needed
without expanding the prototype assignment into product delivery.
